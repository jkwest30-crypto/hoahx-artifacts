01

Scope and fee

The work is ordered because each item depends on the one before it. The current build has to be in production before payments can be certified against it, and payments have to be proven before anyone can sign off on the product as a whole. The test suite is corrected and wired into deployment in week one, before the first release, so every deploy after that is checked automatically, and defects are fixed as verification finds them rather than held for the end.

The phone apps run as a second track alongside. The web app is made phone-ready in the first two weeks, the shell is built in week three while NMI's live approval is pending, and both apps are submitted in week four against payment flows already verified in NMI's test environment. The schedule assumes five to six hours of build time a day, with independent work running in parallel.

05

Ownership and what happens next

Three things worth spelling out plainly, beyond the day-to-day scope above.

01Who owns the code, and who controls the accountsEverything we build is HOAhx's, committed to your own repositories as we go — nothing sits only on our machines. As each payment stage is paid, the work behind it is fully yours; nothing is held back until the very end. You keep administrator control of every account this touches — Firebase, Google Cloud, NMI, Apple, and Google Play — throughout and after the engagement. At completion, you get the documentation to run, back up, and restore everything without us.
02If either of us needs to end this earlyYou pay for the work completed, plus the hours already spent on whatever's in progress at $75 per hour. Everything delivered and paid for is handed over within a business week, and account access moves to you at the same time — not before you've paid for the work behind it.
03If NMI, Apple, or Google says no, rather than just taking a whileIf one of them rejects an application outright, for reasons that have nothing to do with our work — a business decision on their end, for example — a milestone built correctly and delivered on time still counts as done, and we agree in writing on how the rest of the schedule adjusts. If the rejection instead points to a real mistake in our work, we fix it free, the same as any other defect.
