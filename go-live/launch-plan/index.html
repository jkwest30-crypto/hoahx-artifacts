Scope

What we're building

Production readiness
  • The automated test suite corrected so a passing test means a working feature, and wired into deployment so a failure stops a release. In place before the first deploy.
  • Nine broken upload paths fixed and verified in a browser; all 124 pages checked for the database errors that only appear once a real user hits them.
  • The tested build deployed to staging, then production. Internal reports taken off the public site.
  • Defects fixed as verification finds them, weeks one through five, not held for the end.
Payments through NMI
  • Each community becomes its own merchant, set up through NMI with approval arriving automatically.
  • Homeowners pay assessments by card, bank account, Apple Pay, or Google Pay, one time or on autopay against the community's dues schedule.
  • Refunds, returned bank payments, and disputes update the ledger on their own. Treasurers see deposits.
  • A real payment, autopay charge, refund, and dispute run through NMI's live system before sign-off.
  • Each community pays its HOAhx subscription inside HOAhx by bank account or card, with recurring charges, named billing contacts, and a past-due schedule. Built in weeks four and five, after the homeowner payment flows are proven.
Security and protection
  • Two-factor sign-in for staff and board roles, a stronger password floor with breach screening, and a fresh password before issuing a refund. Residents stay on an opt-in path.
  • Abuse protection enforcing, a spending cap and budget alert that reach a person, limits on join-code guessing, and standard browser security protections.
  • Error alerting within minutes, uptime checks on the site and the payment connection, daily backups, and one restore actually rehearsed.
HOAhx on iPhone and Android
  • The existing app inside a native shell, so every feature ships to both stores from one codebase. Free to download; sign-in or a community invitation required.
  • Homeowners, board members, and self-managed admins use it. Management companies stay on desktop.
  • Push notifications, camera for requests, Face ID or fingerprint sign-in, invitation links that open the app, and the NMI payment form working inside it.
  • Store listings, screenshots, privacy disclosures, and a demo community for the reviewers. One round of store-requested changes included.

How the work is ordered

Each piece depends on the one before it. The test suite is corrected and wired into deployment first, so the very first production release is checked and every release after it is too. The current build then goes to production, payments are built against NMI's test environment and then proven with real money, and only then is the web product signed off. The phone apps run alongside: the web app is made phone-ready in the first two weeks, the shell is built in week three while NMI's live approval is pending, and both apps are submitted in week four. The community subscription billing follows in weeks four and five, once the homeowner payment flows are proven.

A from-scratch native app would mean rebuilding most of the screens and maintaining two versions of HOAhx forever. The shell reuses everything already built and tested and still gives residents notifications, the camera, and biometric sign-in. The web parts of the app update over the air; only changes to the shell itself go back through Apple and Google.

Boundaries

  • A second round of store-review changes. One round is included. A further round, if a store asks for it, is at $75 an hour, quoted first.
  • Management-company screens redesigned for phones. Community managers keep the full desktop experience. The apps serve homeowners, board members, and self-managed admins.

Glossary

Terms used on this page

NMIThe payment processor handling homeowner assessment payments and each community's HOAhx subscription. Each community is set up as its own merchant under HOAhx; HOAhx itself is a merchant too, for the subscription charge.
Community subscriptionWhat a community pays HOAhx for the software, billed inside HOAhx through NMI by bank account or card. Homeowners never pay HOAhx.
SandboxNMI's test environment. Everything works like production, but with test cards and no real money.
UnderwritingNMI's review of a community before it can take real payments. Usually days, sometimes weeks, and outside our control.
ACH / bank paymentA payment drawn directly from a checking account. Cheaper than cards, but takes days to settle and can be returned.
AutopayA saved card or bank account charged automatically on the community's dues schedule.
Two-factor sign-inA second step at sign-in, such as a code on your phone, required for staff and board roles because those accounts can move money.
Decisions registerThe list of 111 rules the software applies by default, each shown with what it does today, for you to confirm or change. How the rest of it gets worked through is one of the things Monday's MVP discussion settles.
MVPThe set of features the product has to have on day one. What belongs in it, and what waits until after launch, is what Monday September 14 decides.
Production environmentThe real environment the product will run on, as opposed to the test copy. A build being on it is not the same as the product being launched: HOAhx stays closed until Oct 9.
StagingA private copy of the production environment where a build is checked before it is deployed.
Go / no-goA written recommendation on whether the product is ready to launch, with the evidence behind it.
Native shellThe iPhone and Android app wrapper that runs the existing HOAhx app and adds phone features like notifications and the camera.
TestFlight / internal testingApple's and Google's ways of installing an app on your phone before it's public.
Store reviewApple's and Google's approval process for new apps. Typically days; a first submission is sometimes sent back once with requested changes.
D-U-N-S numberA business identifier Apple requires for organization accounts. HOAhx already has one, so Apple enrollment can start right away.
