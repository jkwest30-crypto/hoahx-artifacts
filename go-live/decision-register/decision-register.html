How these were found. 221 items: your Core list of September 14, 2026 (updated September 15), the features your demo shows, and 109 decisions found on September 4 by reading the code, plus six follow-up questions raised since. Each decision was derived from the code that enforces it — Cloud Functions, security rules, and the role model — as part of a review that produced 897 requirements across 18 domains. On September 4, 2026 they were brought into line with the launch proposal, the launch plan, and the NMI Integration Plan: payments are being built on NMI, so the payment items describe what that build will do unless told otherwise, and items the proposal already settles are not listed.

The three groups. In the release is what the launch delivers: your Core list, anything that closes a security or store requirement, and defects against a Core feature. Candidate is worth keeping and is ranked, not scheduled: one comes into the release only when the build is running far enough ahead to carry it. Not planned is declined, with the reason on the item; nothing is ever deleted, so it can come back. Proven by names the test that will show the item works.

What your marks do. Proposed is what the build will do unless you say otherwise; Keep as-is means proceed with it. Planned means the decision is made and is in the build; it is set from the record when the register is updated, never by a click. On an item already Planned, a Change does not undo it — the work in flight carries on and your change becomes its own item, linked to this one, which you then rank. On a Candidate every mark is ranking: Change means sooner, Keep as-is means fine where it sits, Discuss means talk. Waiting on you marks the 17 items that block the payment build and the live payment test; everything else can be left alone. The D-numbers are the ones used in our own tracking, so either number works in a conversation.

Marks and notes save to a shared register that everyone reviewing this page sees, so nothing needs to be sent back: what you mark here is read straight off the register and recorded. Every earlier version of a mark or note is kept, so clearing one by mistake loses nothing.
